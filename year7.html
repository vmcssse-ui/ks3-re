Week 1 is the week beginning Monday 7 September 2026 — the Year 7 induction day on 2 September and the first two days of term fall before it. The year holds 38 teaching weeks and this scheme has 38 lessons, one per week with none spare.

Unit 1 · Weeks 1–8 · 8 lessons

Beliefs

What RE is and why we study it, then belief against fact and opinion, the question of God, and the artefacts and symbols believers use to show what they hold true.

Why study RE?

Lesson 7–11 September #w1

The first RE lesson of secondary school: what Religious Education is, and why it is worth studying when 7 of the world's 8 billion people hold a religious belief. Sets the expectations students will work to all year.

Note First RE lesson of Year 7 — seating plan, expectations and the Learning Journey are all set up here.

Week321 Sep

What is a belief?

Lesson 21–25 September #w3

The distinction the whole unit rests on: a belief is accepted as true, a fact can be proven, an opinion says how someone feels. Students sort statements into the three and learn why people can hold different beliefs honestly.

▶Lesson deck▤Worksheet — belief, fact, opinion▤Lesson plan
Week428 Sep

Is seeing believing?

Lesson 28 Sep – 2 Oct #w4

Optical illusions open the question of whether the eye can be trusted, then the lesson turns to theism, atheism and agnosticism, and to the ways belief is shown in practice — Christian prayer and Jummah prayer.

▶Lesson deck▤Lesson plan
Week55 Oct

Is there a God?

Lesson 5–9 October #w5

Do people have good reasons for believing in God, or for not believing? Students weigh evidence, meet the theist, the atheist and the agnostic as three positions rather than three camps, and argue their own view.

Religious artefacts

Lesson 12–16 October #w6

Objects that carry belief: rosary beads, the crucifix, the prayer mat and the compass that points towards Mecca. Students handle the artefacts, then explain what each one tells you about the believer who uses it.

Religious symbols

Lesson 19–23 October #w7

The cross, Aum, the Dharma Wheel, the Star of David and the crescent moon and star. Students learn to read a symbol as a compressed belief — and to see why a sign and a symbol are not the same thing.

Diwali

Lesson 16–20 November #w9

Diwali, the festival of lights, told through Rama and Sita's return from exile and Hanuman's part in it. Students learn why the divas are lit and what light defeating darkness means to a Hindu family.
